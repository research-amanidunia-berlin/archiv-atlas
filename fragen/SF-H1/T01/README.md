# SF-H1-T01

By what agreed criteria should governments distinguish legitimate research use of publicly available AI outputs from distillation that systematically undermines another nation's safety investments? Will they publish a shared, narrow definition of which model-output uses each of them considers a violation, and keep a shared, auditable register of verified unauthorised attempts or of licensed distillation? How otherwise does each government show that it is enforcing the agreement rather than using the accusation selectively?

Part of the collected question [SF-H1](../../../fragen/SF-H1/).

## Formed from these original questions

- **origin-01**: What criteria should both governments agree on to distinguish between legitimate use of publicly available AI outputs for research and model distillation that systematically undermines another nation's safety investments?
- **origin-02**: Distillation of frontier US or Chinese models by laboratories in the other country is now part of the talks; will the two governments publish a shared, narrow definition of which model-output uses (e.g. synthetic fine-tuning data, capability-eval harvesting) they each consider a violation, or will the term remain a unilateral accusation?
- **origin-25**: If both governments agree on a shared definition of unauthorized distillation (as asked in origin-02), will they also maintain a shared, auditable log or registry of verified unauthorized attempts — or of licensed, legitimate distillation — and if not, how does either side prove it is enforcing the agreement rather than using the accusation selectively?
