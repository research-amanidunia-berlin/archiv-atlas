# SF-T3-T02

Will labs that publish a measure of how well their models can be monitored and a measure of autonomous AI research publish both jointly on one calendar? And will a standards body make not either measure alone but their crossing trajectories (declining monitorability alongside rising autonomous research) a trigger for review, as an early warning of lost human oversight before the onset of self-improvement can be named?

Part of the collected question [SF-T3](../../../fragen/SF-T3/).

## Formed from these original questions

- **humans-37**: Pachocki writes that models "become much smarter even without using verbalized reasoning at all" (A28) and that reliance on CoT monitoring is "progressively diminishing" (A25), while OpenAI separately proposes measuring "the amount of autonomous research happening within an AI company" (ST26). ST26's metric is behavioral — it counts what researchers do. What nobody has proposed is the pairing: a measured decline in monitorability (A25) alongside a measured rise in autonomous research (ST26) is the closest thing the material offers to an operational early-warning signature for the loss of human oversight, before RSI onset (#106) can even be named. Will the labs that publish both numbers — OpenAI publishes a monitorability metric (g-mean²); Anthropic publishes its RSI mini-test — publish them jointly on one calendar, and will the standards body treat their crossing trajectories, not either alone, as a review trigger?
