# SF-M2-T09

Before the reporting channel relies on incident reports from labs as triggers, will each government state which lab-internal decisions about disclosing incidents externally it will treat as inputs it cannot audit? And is a lab's unexplained non-disclosure, where a security delay permits it, treated as silence or as a reportable omission?

Part of the collected question [SF-M2](../../../fragen/SF-M2/).

## Formed from these original questions

- **hotline-38**: OpenAI's F10 says "serious safety, security and misalignment incidents should be shared with the US federal government, and we are working to propose reporting mechanisms." Amodei at the Council proposes "a notification system for AI incidents that are significant to global security" (L292) — a government-to-government channel. Between them sits an unexamined relay: under the proposed architecture, a cross-border hotline report may originate in a lab's internal disclosure process (F7–F24) whose criteria were written unilaterally, revised at will (F25), and whose disclosure decisions end at lab leadership (F24). Before the hotline relies on lab-originated incident reports as triggers, will both governments state which lab-internal disclosure decisions they will treat as inputs they cannot audit — and would a lab's unexplained non-disclosure (permitted under F22's security delays) be treated as silence or as a reportable omission?
