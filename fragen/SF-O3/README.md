## Subquestions

- [SF-O3-T01](../../fragen/SF-O3/T01/): When a safety report, a filing to the reporting channel, a model card (a model description) or a training-dataset manifest is published, which public cryptographic evidence (signed manifests, hash chains, registries that can only be added to) lets outsiders verify that the document has not been edited since publication, and who runs the infrastructure for it so that no one can revoke those checks alone?
- [SF-O3-T02](../../fragen/SF-O3/T02/): When long-lived incident records, safety attestations, AI-agent identities and audit signatures outlive today's cryptography, which rules for migration to quantum-resistant methods, key rotation and the continued verification of older evidence should everyone adopt without invalidating previously accepted evidence?
- [SF-O3-T03](../../fragen/SF-O3/T03/): Will the official verbatim record of a meeting be published, and until then what status should readers give the automatic transcript that everyone quotes? This was asked about the UN Security Council meeting of 23 September 2026 on artificial intelligence and international security (record S/PV.10228).
- [SF-O3-T04](../../fragen/SF-O3/T04/): Which text governs in a bilateral record when the official record, a lab's own text of remarks “as delivered” and the automatic transcript differ on something decision-relevant? And will the governments agree that no incident classification or quotation from the meeting enters documents for the reporting channel or for standards until checked against the official record and the original-language audio? This was asked about OpenAI CEO Sam Altman's remarks at the UN Security Council on 23 September 2026.
- [SF-O3-T05](../../fragen/SF-O3/T05/): Whose account of what the other side said counts as the record when a frontier AI model on one side prepares or summarises the other side's negotiating positions, and would either side accept a model-mediated summary as a basis for agreement?
