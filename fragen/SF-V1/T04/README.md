# SF-V1-T04

How is the monitorability of reasoning measured with a shared, reproducible benchmark before it fails, and how can one test reliably whether visible reasoning faithfully reflects what the model actually does, even when models learn to optimise their reasoning steps? And if monitoring results influence which training samples or checkpoints are kept, how do you test whether that selection favours less detectable misbehaviour rather than less misbehaviour, even when the reasoning text is never directly graded?

Part of the collected question [SF-V1](../../../fragen/SF-V1/).

- **trust-05**: The bet is to leave reasoning unsupervised so it stays monitorable (A24). Can it be tested before it fails, not only in hindsight?
- **trust-06**: How can labs develop empirical evaluations for chain-of-thought faithfulness that remain reliable even when models learn to optimize their reasoning steps?
- **trust-07**: What would a shared, reproducible monitorability benchmark look like, so "progressively diminishing" becomes a number rather than a feeling?
- **trust-32**: If monitoring results influence which training samples or checkpoints are retained, how will you test whether that selection favors less detectable misbehavior rather than less misbehavior, even when reasoning text is never directly graded?
